Paul's Take

AI Is Y2K Times Ten Million

Venture Builders FundNovember 20263 min read

Remember Y2K? Our General Partner Paul Kim does.

In the late nineties, the warning was that when the clocks hit 2000, banking would stop working and planes would fall from the sky. Anyone who knew the technology knew it wasn't true. But the fear was useful. It drove a wave of spending on consultants and systems to fix a collapse that never came.

A story with no clock

Paul's view is that the AI doom narrative works the same way, with one big difference. Y2K had a deadline. On January 1st, everyone found out whether it was real.

The AI version has no clock. It can stay open-ended forever, which makes it a very useful marketing story for anyone selling the fix.

The threat it hides

His point isn't that nothing can go wrong. It's that the dramatic threat distracts from the quieter one: handing over our expertise, one workflow at a time. That is the risk worth planning around, and it is one each of us controls.

For founders pitching AI products, and for investors evaluating them, it is a useful filter. Ask whether a pitch is selling a real problem, or selling the fear of one.
