The Real AI Threat Is Death by a Thousand Cuts

Venture Builders FundNovember 20263 min read

Most conversations about AI risk go straight to the dramatic version: machines that turn on us, jobs that vanish overnight. Our General Partner Paul Kim worries about something much quieter.

He calls it death by a thousand cuts. Every email, calendar and workflow we hand to an AI platform teaches it a little more about how we work. Do that with the routine parts of a job and you get your time back. Do it with your core expertise and, over time, the tool learns to replace the very thing that makes you valuable.

Where the line sits

Paul is not anti-AI. He uses it, and he thinks investors who ignore it will fall behind. His test is simple. Let it handle the work that isn't yours. Watch closely when it starts doing the work that is.

“When it starts doing the work for you, that's when you've crossed the line. Automate things that are just non-proprietary and keep your value.”

Paul Kim, General Partner, Venture Builders Fund

He applies that test to his own investment process. AI can do the background research on a sector in minutes, and he is happy to let it. But, in his words, "I will never give exactly how I analyze companies to the machine."

Why it matters for founders and programs

The same test works anywhere expertise is the product. For founders, it means automating the admin and the desk research while keeping the judgment calls, the customer insight and the product taste in human hands. For accelerators, it means letting AI run scheduling and first-pass research, while keeping how you pick founders, and what you teach them, firmly your own.

The dramatic risks make better headlines. The thousand small handovers are the ones most of us are making right now.
